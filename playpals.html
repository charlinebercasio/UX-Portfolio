01The problem

Parents were doing subscription chores on a laptop at 11pm.

Playpals sends stage-based toy boxes to families with young kids. Managing the subscription (previewing the next box, swapping a toy, pausing) lived on the website, and the site offered little reason to come back between deliveries.

For parents

Swaps meant emailing support. Advice online felt judgy. Nothing helped at 3am, when the real questions come up.

For the business

Subscribers lapse from friction, clutter and guilt at the cancel step, not only from cost.

The opportunity

Make the chores two taps, then give parents a reason to open the app between boxes.

How might we
How might we help parents kill subscription chores in two taps, then reward them for staying?
02Research

Toy overwhelm is universal. Swap friction is close behind.

8 semi-structured interviews (45 min, remote) with parents of kids aged 0–4: 5 subscribers and 3 lapsed. Recruited from 41 screener responses; journey walk-throughs of their last box plus a think-aloud on the current site. 212 notes, affinity-mapped into 5 themes.

1
Toy overwhelm · 8/8

Every parent felt buried in toys. The app had to reduce decisions, not add them.

2
Swap friction · 7/8

Changing what's in the next box meant digging through the site or emailing support.

3
Judgy advice · 6/8

"Every parenting site makes me feel like I'm already behind." Sources had to feel calm and credible.

4
"Is this normal?" · 6/8

Late-night development questions with nowhere trustworthy to ask.

5
Guilt at cancel · 5/8

Lapsed parents kept boxes they no longer needed because cancelling felt bad.

6
Nap-time sessions · 8/8

Everything happens one-handed, in minutes, on a phone.

"I googled 'is throwing food normal' at 3am. I would've paid for one trustworthy answer."
P1, mom of one (13 months)

05Key decisions

What the research changed.

01

Every core job in two taps or fewer

Confirm, swap and pause all live within two taps of home, sized for one-handed nap-time sessions.

Why8 of 8 parents managed the subscription in stolen minutes on a phone.
02

Keep / Swap on each toy, in place

The swap is a toggle on the toy itself. A flagged duplicate turns from red dashed to mint, and parents never lose their spot in the pack.

Why7 of 8 hit swap friction, and testing showed they expected the control on the toy, not on a separate page.
03

A learn hub that is never judgy, always sourced

Stage-based articles open with a gist card, then sections that rise in. Every piece is framed as reviewed, calm guidance.

Why6 of 8 found parenting advice judgy. Trust is the product.
04

3am questions are first-class

"Is this normal?" became a forum topic, and Ask Playpals gives sourced takes inside threads and in its own chat.

Why6 of 8 searched development questions late at night with nowhere trustworthy to land.
05

Guilt-free pause

Pause, keep toys, switch to a free plan or cancel, all on one screen, with no hoops.

Why5 of 8 felt guilt at cancel. Making leaving easy makes coming back easier.
06Craft

Small motion, with a job to do.

Six micro-interactions translate the Playpals brand voice into motion, and each one answers a real question.

Primary press

A .96 squish in 120ms confirms the tap before the screen changes.

Keep ⇄ Swap in place

The row changes where it stands, so parents never lose their spot.

Toys with a wiggle

Pack tiles bounce in a 150ms cascade, Playpals' playful voice in motion.

Cycle progress

The bar fills on arrival, so "when's our swap?" is answered before anyone asks.

Like with a pop

Heart and count change together, so the club feels alive without a single notification.

Sections that rise in

Long reads arrive in bites a tired parent can take.

Accessibility

  • 44px+ tap targets and high-contrast type, designed with Rosa in mind
  • Larger text settings tested on every screen
  • All motion respects reduced-motion settings
  • Loops pause when scrolled out of view

AI, used carefully

  • Ask Playpals answers by intent: prose for advice, tiles for suggestions
  • Sourced takes in threads, never a replacement for a pediatrician
  • Prompt pills that never run out, so parents aren't stuck at a blank box
  • AI-assisted prototyping to test real conversations, not mockups
