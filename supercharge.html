01The problem

Fit is the question behind almost every bra purchase.

Help existed in pieces, a size guide here and an associate there, but it wasn’t timed to the moments shoppers actually doubted. And two brands needed to say it in two voices.

For shoppers

  • Sizes vary by style, so knowing your size isn’t knowing it in this style
  • Hesitation online, armfuls of “just in case” sizes in the fitting room
  • Second-guessing after purchase, once it’s actually worn

For the business

  • Fit help scattered across channels, not timed to doubt
  • VS and PINK customers trust different voices
  • Research needed to outlive a single project
02Research

We went where fit actually happens.

With UX Research and my UX lead, I ran in-store studies: shoppers observed from rack to room to register, and associates interviewed about the questions they answer every day.

Explored and dropped

✕
A fit quiz before browsing

Gating the catalog behind questions slowed shoppers who already knew their size. Help moved to the moment of doubt instead.

✕
A chat pop-up on every product page

Always-on prompts trained shoppers to ignore them. Trigger points show help only where the research found hesitation.

05Tonality

Same help, two voices: VS ≠ PINK.

VS and PINK customers trust differently, so every trigger point got guidance for both voices.

PINK

"Heads up: this one runs a lil snug in the band · most people size up!"
