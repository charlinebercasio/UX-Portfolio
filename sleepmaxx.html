Sleepmaxx: an AI sleep coach that works with your chaos, not against it.

Most sleep apps assume you control your schedule. Students and shift workers don't. Sleepmaxx reads tomorrow's first commitment and tonight's reality, then coaches the best sleep possible inside it · warm, a little funny, never preachy.

NEW FEATURE · BRAIN DUMP

Get it out of your head, then sleep

Racing thoughts are a common reason people can't fall asleep even when the plan is perfect. The Journal tab, also linked from wind-down, gives them somewhere to go.

Prompts, not a blank page. "Tomorrow I need to…", "on my mind…", "one good thing" get a tired brain started in one tap.
Park it, don't solve it. One tap sorts the dump into tomorrow's to-dos (with a reminder after the alarm), parked thoughts, and a good thing to end on.
Shows it's working. Past dumps sit next to how fast you fell asleep, so the habit proves itself.

Grounded in sleep research: a 2018 Baylor University study found that writing a specific to-do list before bed helped people fall asleep faster than writing about tasks already done.

01The problem

Sleep advice assumes control most young people don’t have.

Students and shift workers know the rules. Their schedules just don’t let them follow them, and most apps punish them for it.

What exists

  • “Go to bed at 10, wake at 6”: one fixed schedule
  • Trackers that score last night after the fact
  • Streaks and red bars that make a bad night feel like failure
  • Reminders that ignore a closing shift or an 8am

What users actually live

  • Shifts that end at 1am, classes that start at 8
  • Nights out that are worth it, and a morning that still happens
  • Exam weeks where every night is short
  • Apps deleted after the first guilt trip
How might we
…coach the best possible sleep inside the schedule someone actually has, so a chaotic night becomes a plan instead of a failure?
02Research

The screen after a bad night decides whether the app survives.

A survey, follow-up interviews and an audit of how sleep and habit apps handle missed nights.

Survey

75 students and shift workers: schedule shape, sleep apps tried, why they stopped.

Interviews

15 follow-ups walking through their last rough week, night by night.

App audit

Onboarding and “missed night” states in popular sleep and habit apps: where tone turns into guilt.

What we learned

1
They know the rules, they can’t apply them

The gap is applying sleep hygiene to a schedule they don’t control.

2
Tomorrow’s first thing sets tonight

The time of tomorrow’s first commitment is the real anchor, not a fixed bedtime.

3
Shame is the churn moment

Guilt after a bad night is when people delete the app.

4
Think in weeks, not nights

One short night is recoverable; planning recovery across the week turns stress into strategy.

05Key decisions

Five choices that shaped the coach.

01

Anchor to tomorrow, not a bedtime

The calendar’s first commitment drives the window, wake time and wind-down; the plan is rebuilt every evening.

Trade-offcalendar access is a big ask, so it’s optional; a single “first thing tomorrow” time works too.
02

One honest question beats a setup wizard

“Working a closing shift tonight?” instantly rewrites the window and the coach’s note.

Why it mattersvalue on night one, with no tracking history needed.
03

A coach voice with rules

Acknowledge, do the math, give one or two moves, end warm. No “you should have.”

Guardrailnot medical advice; ongoing insomnia patterns get a gentle “worth talking to a doctor.”
04

The week, not the night

Sleep debt is a recoverable number with planned days ahead; exam week mode spreads recovery out.

Detailshort nights are warm amber, never red; planned nights are dashed, so the future looks editable.
05

A brain dump that sorts itself

Prompt-led, two minutes, sorted on save into to-dos, parked thoughts and one good thing.

Trade-offno mood tracking or streaks; it stays a utility, not another habit to keep up.
06Tone guide

In behavior change, copy is the interface.

The same plan reads as support or judgment depending on one sentence, so every coach reply follows these rules.

MomentNotInstead
After a night out“You missed your sleep goal. Streak lost.”“Been there, no shame, just math: you’re ~2h short.”
Can’t fall asleep“Avoid screens 2 hours before bed.”“Classic wired-tired. Phone across the room, lights warm.”
Short night planned“Warning: under 6 hours.”“Short night, let’s make it count: nap blocked at 2pm.”
