Solenne: an editorial apparel store where the fastest path to the bag is also the prettiest one.

A full e-commerce experience for a fictional women's apparel brand, designed from scratch: shop tabs, listing and product pages, fit finder, bag, checkout, orders, wishlist and a 3-tier loyalty program, then adapted for mobile web and a native app. This case study covers the thinking behind it: the decisions, the versions I dropped, and the edge cases.

01The brief

Premium brands slow you down to feel premium. Solenne shouldn't have to.

Editorial apparel sites tend to trade speed for mood: big imagery, hidden sizes, extra steps. Fast-fashion sites do the reverse. The brief I set myself was a store that feels like a magazine but shops like a utility, for a customer who knows her style and is short on time.

Who she is

Late 20s to 40s, buys a few considered pieces a season, shops on her phone in between things and finishes on desktop.

What gets in her way

Unsure about fit, sizes that are sold out only after she clicks, surprise shipping costs, and loyalty points she never sees until the receipt.

What the business needs

Higher add-to-bag from the listing page, fewer fit-related returns, and a loyalty program people actually use to come back.

How might we
How might we make a premium apparel store feel effortless to buy from without losing the editorial calm that makes it feel premium?
02Principles

Four rules I designed against.

Before any screens, I wrote the rules every decision had to pass. They became the tiebreaker whenever two ideas were both good.

1
Decide where the doubt is

Fit help sits beside the sizes, shipping progress sits in the bag, and points show on the product page. Help belongs at the exact moment of hesitation.

2
Never a dead end

Every empty, sold-out or error state offers a next step: clear filters, suggested searches, notify me, points to go.

3
Quiet chrome, loud product

UI elements are hairlines, small caps and frosted panels, so the garment and the type do the talking.

4
Loyalty is part of the flow

Points show up on the product page, in the bag, in checkout and on the receipt, not in a separate rewards page nobody opens.

05Key decisions

Six decisions that shaped the store.

01

Fit finder answers one question: what size should I buy?

Two inputs: your usual size and how you like things to fit. Each garment carries a fit scale (runs small → oversized), so the recommendation moves up or down from your usual and says why in one sentence.

WhyFit uncertainty is the moment shoppers stall, and the top reason apparel gets returned. A recommendation that explains itself builds trust where a size chart builds homework.
02

A bag drawer, not a bag page

Adding from the product page opens a drawer over the page you're on, with free-shipping progress and the points you'll earn. Close it and you're exactly where you were.

Trade-offA drawer has less room for upsells. I kept it to one shipping nudge rather than a carousel, because the goal of the drawer is confidence, not more items.
03

Checkout on one page, with steps that collapse

Contact, delivery, shipping and payment open one at a time. Finished steps collapse into a one-line summary with Edit. Express pay is first, and the order summary stays pinned beside the form.

WhyPeople lose confidence when they can't see what they're paying for. Keeping the total, promo and points visible the whole time removes the "wait, what's this charge?" moment.
04

Loyalty shows up in every step

Muse, Icon and Atelier tiers. Points show on the product page ("earn 192 pts"), in the bag, as a toggle at checkout, and on the confirmation with progress to the next tier.

WhyPoints that only appear on a receipt feel like an afterthought. Showing them before purchase turns them into a reason to buy, and redeeming them is one switch.
05

The wishlist is a pressure-free save

A heart on every image and on the product page, the same state everywhere, a count in the header, and in the app an alert when a saved piece goes on sale.

WhySaving is how people shortlist without committing. If saving feels heavy (sign-in walls, popups), they don't save, and you lose the signal for when to bring them back.
06

Type with two voices

Bodoni Moda for the SOLENNE wordmark only, Playfair Display for headlines, and DM Sans for everything you have to read or tap. I compared six serif pairings before landing on this.

WhyA high-contrast Bodoni is beautiful at logo size but brittle in long headlines. Playfair keeps the editorial feel and stays readable, and a clean sans keeps prices, sizes and forms easy to scan.
06Deep dive · quick add

Three versions to make "quick" actually quick.

Quick add is the biggest shortcut on the listing page, and the easiest one to get wrong. It has to be fast, honest about stock, and not cover the product you're trying to look at.

QUICK ADD +
v1 · dropped
Full-bleed black strip

Covered a third of the garment and read as "sale bin", not editorial. Still needed a second step to pick a size.

+
v2 · dropped
Floating + button

Quiet, but it opened a size modal on top of the grid. Two clicks and a context switch for something called "quick".

QUICK ADDXSSMLXL
v3 · shipped
Inset size panel

Sizes live in a frosted panel inset 12px from the image edge. One click adds the exact size. Sold-out sizes stay visible but struck through.

Why inset, not edge to edge

Pulling the panel 12px in from the image edges lets the garment frame it, so it reads as part of the image rather than a sticker on top. The frosted background keeps the photo visible behind it, and the panel slides up only on hover or keyboard focus.

Why sizes, not a button

The size is the only decision quick add needs. Showing sizes directly turns "Quick add → pick size → confirm" into one click, and shows availability before the shopper commits to opening the product.

Accessibility

  • Every icon button has a label that changes with its state ("Save to" / "Remove from wishlist")
  • Sold-out sizes are disabled and announced, not just faded out
  • Quick add opens on keyboard focus as well as hover
  • Errors are inline text, never color alone

Interaction details

  • Images zoom in slightly on hover, slow and subtle
  • Quick add slides up, and toasts confirm without blocking
  • Checkout steps collapse to summaries, so progress is visible
  • Garment illustrations stand in for photography in this concept
