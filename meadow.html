Meadow: a health savings marketplace that makes "is this eligible?" a non-question.

HSA members have tax-free money to spend on care, but most don't know what qualifies, worry about getting it wrong, and lose receipts. Meadow is an end-to-end commerce experience inside an HSA app: discover, search, compare, check out with HSA funds, and never think about receipts again.

01The problem

The money is there. The confidence to spend it isn't.

HSA members have tax-free dollars for care, but unclear rules, fear of penalties and lost receipts keep them from using them, and send them shopping elsewhere.

For members

  • Eligibility rules are unintuitive: sunscreen yes, self-tanner no, vitamins only with a doctor's note
  • Fear of a non-qualified purchase makes people avoid spending at all
  • Receipts get lost, and members are the ones responsible for keeping them
  • HSA vs FSA confusion: many think unused HSA money disappears

For the business

  • Members shop elsewhere, then return only to upload receipts
  • Partner services need a trusted place to be discovered
  • Engagement drops between open enrollment and tax season
  • Promotions must feel helpful, never like ads for health spending
How might we
…help members spend their health dollars with total confidence, from the first search to the receipt they'll need at tax time?
02Discovery

Uncertainty, not price, is what stops the purchase.

Three inputs, then one cross-functional workshop to turn findings into an MVP.

Member interviews

12 HSA holders: last health purchase, how they checked eligibility, where receipts live.

AI-assisted synthesis

Clustered 398 app reviews and forum threads with AI, then validated every theme by hand against real quotes.

Competitive audit

Eligibility labels, checkout and receipt handling across HSA/FSA stores and big-box retailers.

What we learned

1
Uncertainty, not price, stops the purchase

Members abandon when they can't tell if something qualifies. The answer has to live on the result, not in an FAQ.

2
"Will I have enough?" gets asked at checkout

Balance belongs in the payment step, with what's left after the purchase.

3
Personalization needs a reason

In health, an unexplained recommendation feels like surveillance. "Based on your past orders" turns creepy into helpful.

4
The experience ends at tax time, not checkout

Saved receipts and reorder reminders bring members back between enrollment and tax season.

Explored and dropped

✕
A separate "HSA store" tab

Split the catalog into eligible and everything else. Dropped: two catalogs means two mental models, and members still wouldn't know why an item qualifies.

✕
An eligibility quiz before shopping

Ask members what they need, then show eligible options. Dropped: it adds a step before value, and eligibility is a property of the product, not the person.

05Key decisions

Five patterns, built to scale across every partner.

01

An eligibility badge system

Three states on every result and product page: ✓ eligible, ⓘ needs a doctor's note, or not eligible, each with a plain-language "why."

Scales toevery partner catalog. Eligibility comes from data, so merchandising never hand-labels products.
02

Balance-aware checkout

HSA card preselected, with available balance and "left after this" shown at the moment of decision, plus a note that the receipt saves itself.

Nextsplit payment when a cart exceeds the balance or mixes eligible and non-eligible items.
03

Partner comparison cards

Telehealth partners compared on what members asked about: price, wait, format, and whether they can prescribe.

Scales tovision, dental and therapy partners with the same card; only the attributes change.
04

Transparent personalization and promotions

"Picked for you" always answers "why these?" (past orders and plan, never claims). Partner offers carry a visible label.

Trade-offlabels may lower clicks slightly, but trust is the long-term conversion lever in health and money.
05

Post-purchase as lifecycle

Order tracking, an auto-saved receipt, and a reorder or follow-up reminder turn one purchase into a relationship.

Hooksreorder nudges, tax-time export, a year-to-date tax-free total.

07Craft

Accessible by default, AI where it speeds things up.

Accessibility · WCAG 2.2 AA

  • Eligibility uses icon + text + color, never color alone
  • Badges and balances announced clearly to screen readers
  • 44px+ targets for every primary action, mobile-first
  • Eligibility rules in plain language, no tax jargon

AI in my process

  • First-pass clustering of reviews and forum posts, then checked by hand
  • This working prototype was built with AI-assisted prototyping
  • Copy variants for eligibility explanations, edited for tone
  • Every decision traced back to member needs, not the tool
