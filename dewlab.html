Dewlab: booking, intake and aftercare for a modern med-spa, in one calm app.

Med-spa clients book through DMs and clunky widgets, fill out PDF intake forms at the front desk, and lose the aftercare card in their bag. Dewlab turns the whole visit cycle into four calm moments: book, check in, care, rebook.

TRY IT YOURSELF

Book a treatment, start to finish

This phone doesn't auto-play. Tap through it like a real client:

  1. Tap Book in the bottom bar
  2. Filter by category, then pick a treatment · try Skin consult or Hydrafacial
  3. Choose a day and an open time · full slots are struck through
  4. Answer the two-question skin check · try Yes to see the reassurance
  5. Tap Book appointment, then Back home to see your new visit

Then explore the rest: reschedule or add your visit to the calendar from home, check off aftercare steps, log a photo, message Maya, or rebook in one tap.

01The problem

The treatment is luxurious. Everything around it feels like paperwork.

Booking in DMs, clipboard intake forms, a paper aftercare card and rebooking that depends on memory: the visit cycle around the treatment works against both the client and the studio.

For clients

  • Booking happens in DMs or a widget that hides prices until checkout
  • Intake is a multi-page form on a clipboard in the lobby
  • Aftercare is a paper card or a quick “no retinol for a few days”
  • Rebooking depends on remembering, until the glow fades

For the studio

  • Appointments start late while intake happens at the desk
  • Contraindications like recent retinol surface mid-treatment
  • Clients lapse between visits with no timely, personal prompt
  • The brand lives on Instagram, but booking feels nothing like it
How might we
…make every step between treatments feel as cared-for as the treatment itself, while getting the studio what it needs before the client walks in?
02Research

Trust, not polish, is what converts in wellness.

A competitive audit plus conversations with clients and estheticians, synthesized into four insights.

Competitive audit

Booking on ✎ n salon and med-spa platforms and studio sites: taps to book, when price appears, how intake works.

Client conversations

✎ n regular skin-treatment clients: how they book, what they forget, why they lapse.

Esthetician input

✎ n estheticians and owners: what they need before a client arrives, which aftercare gets ignored.

What we learned

1
Price anxiety kills bookings early

Flows that reveal price late feel like a trap, especially to first-timers comparing studios.

2
Only a few intake answers change the treatment

Recent actives and new meds matter today; most history rarely changes.

3
Aftercare fails on day 2, not day 0

Instructions are clear in the chair and forgotten by the next morning.

4
Rebooking is about timing, not discounts

The best moment is when results start to fade, not a generic promo.

A full intake form at every booking

Most history never changes. Asking it once at signup and only the two treatment-changing questions per booking cut the form to seconds.

✕
Aftercare by push notification only

Notifications disappear once read. A checklist on home stays visible, shows progress, and doubles as a photo log for the esthetician.

05Key decisions

Four choices that shaped the app.

01

Price and time on the first screen

Every treatment shows price, duration and what it does before a single tap. Full slots are struck through rather than hidden.

Trade-offshowing price early can scare off browsers, but it filters for clients who’ll book and builds trust with first-timers.
02

A 2-question skin check, not a PDF

History is collected once; each booking asks only what changes the treatment. A “yes” reassures instead of alarming.

Why it mattersthe esthetician sees flags before arrival, and the client never re-types her history.
03

Aftercare as a daily checklist

Steps come from the esthetician’s notes, live on home, and end with a photo that builds a skin journey.

Detailchecking a step fills the progress on home too: one action, visible in two places.
04

Rebook on the skin’s schedule

The prompt arrives when results usually fade, pre-filled with the same esthetician, time and intake answers.

Business angleretention without discount codes; the reminder is useful, so it doesn’t feel like marketing.
06Visual language

Editorial spa, not friendly app.

Dewlab sits next to a playful consumer app in this portfolio, so it was designed to feel deliberately different: type-led, hairlines instead of cards, almost no ornament.

  • 44px+ tap targets for every primary action
  • Plain-language intake, no medical jargon
  • Privacy note at the point of asking, not buried in settings
  • Full slots shown with strike-through and reduced opacity, not color alone
